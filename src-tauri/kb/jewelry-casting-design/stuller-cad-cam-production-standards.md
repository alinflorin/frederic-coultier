# Stuller: CAD/CAM Production Standards (best practices for CAD/CAM services)

Source: https://cdn.hackaday.io/files/1657037089437152/CADCAMProductionStandards.pdf (Stuller.com/CADCAMServices)
Notes transcribed from the PDF text. All dimensions in mm. Keywords: prong, bezel, channel, gypsy, flush, pave, pinpoint, bead, milgrain, shank, wall thickness, opening, pilot hole, divot, raised letters, recessed letters, engraving, casting.

## The basics
- Stones need a minimum clearance of 0.5 mm from the culet to the finger rail.
- Design elements and scroll work: minimum metal thickness 0.5 mm.
- All design elements should overlap each other by 0.15 mm (stronger joints).
- All openings in a design: minimum 0.4 mm, to avoid being filled during casting or causing investment relocation.
- Minimum shank thickness 1.2 mm to 1.7 mm (depending on the shank style shown), because production finishing can remove up to 0.2 mm of metal.
- Parts of a stone: table, crown, girdle, pavilion, culet.

## Prong setting
1. The prong must overlap the girdle of the stone, but the seat for the stone should not cut deeper than 25% of the prong.
2. Typically the culet is visible above the bottom gallery rail.
3. The base of the prong dome should align with the table of the stone, for all set types including pinpoint setting for melee.
4. Minimum distance between gallery rails/bearings: 0.4 mm.
5. The pavilion of the gem should barely rest on the surface of the rail.
- Prong-set cushions: cushion stones with rounded corners can rotate and fall out of a prong setting. Option 1: double prongs. Option 2: gallery rail.

## Shared prong and pinpoint settings
- Shared prongs: stones are suspended and secured by the prongs; prongs are moved into place and finished separately (more time, more skill than pinpoint).
- Pinpoint: stones set in a channel; a beading tool moves and finishes the prongs in one motion (fastest set type).
- Pinpoint melee: minimum channel wall thickness 0.4 mm.
- Pinpoint prongs: minimum prong diameter 0.45 mm.
- Pinpoint spacing, girdle to girdle: minimum 0.15 mm.

## Bezel setting
- As stone size grows, the table rises above the top surface of the bezel (examples at 2.0, 4.0, 7.0 mm).
- Bezel wall thickness: minimum 0.45 mm.

## Bead setting on bezel or channel
- Beads no larger than 0.5 mm and no smaller than 0.4 mm regardless of stone size.
- 70% of the bead should be visible above the top surface of the bezel.
- Bezel wall minimum 0.45 mm.
- Beads must not touch: minimum spacing 0.01 mm.
- Seen from the top, beads must barely touch the gem.

## Channel setting
- Channel cutter width: princess/baguettes 98% of stone, rounds 95%.
- Wall thickness by stone size: stone > 2.5 mm: 0.80 mm; 1.8-2.5 mm: 0.65 mm; < 1.8 mm: 0.50 mm.
- Girdle scale of the gem cutter at 98%.
- Shank profile matters: a curved shank profile can reduce the real channel wall thickness even if the side view looks right; increase channel profile height to compensate.

## Gypsy / flush setting
- Use a gem cutter to remove all metal above the girdle.
- 98% girdle scale.
- Wall/spacing by stone size: < 1.8 mm: 0.35 mm; 1.8-2.5 mm: 0.45 mm; > 2.5 mm: 0.50 mm.

## Pilot holes, divots and openings
- A pilot hole (or any opening) is acceptable if its depth is no more than double its diameter. Example: 1.0 mm diameter x 1.35 mm deep is good; 0.6 mm diameter x 1.35 mm deep is too narrow (casting defects, investment relocation).
- Use a divot instead of a pilot hole when a pilot hole cannot be added (drill after casting).

## Milgrain, surprise gems
- Milgrain on a finger rail: minimum 0.3 mm above the rail.
- Surprise gems: minimum 0.3 mm above the finger rail.

## Hollowed out
- Structural and external walls: minimum 0.8 mm metal.

## Raised letters
- Fillet all edges, round corners (helps casting, avoids investment relocation).
- Minimum metal thickness (stroke) 0.3 mm.
- Height at most 0.6 mm above the surface.
- Spacing between letters at least 0.3 mm.

## Recessed letters
- Fillet the bottom edges, round corners.
- Minimum width 0.3 mm.
- Depth no more than double the width (same rule as openings).
